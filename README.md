# Dissertation_Code:

Financial content on YouTube attracts millions of views daily yet much of it can
mislead rather than inform. This research investigates money making content on
YouTube across a corpus of 84,838 videos derived from the YouNiverse dataset. Using
FASTopic, (a neural topic modelling technique) the analysis derives a data-driven
taxonomy of 4 main financial content strategies and shows how their popularity and the
attention they attract have shifted over time. Furthermore, using a grounded codebook
based on recent literature, a hand-labelled and double-annotated sample of 1,000
videos are designed and used to train a classifier for automated detection of potentially
harmful misleading financial videos. The classifier detects misleading financial YouTube
videos from their title and description alone, reaching strong out of sample accuracy
demonstrated by a macro F1 score of 0.84. Our results reveal that misleading content
concentrates in the trading, online earning and e-commerce strategies, dominating the
views they receive, reaching approximately 45% in the trading, online earning and
e-commerce strategies against 18.5% in entrepreneurship and real estate. To analyse
financial content during and post-pandemic, a second, independent corpus from the
YouTube Commons dataset extends the findings beyond 2019 to 2024 with 14,878
videos, documenting the recent emergence of artificial intelligence (AI) as a money
making theme. Together these results map both the shape and the risks of financial
content on YouTube as they evolve over time.
